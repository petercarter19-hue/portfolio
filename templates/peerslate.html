The Problem

Resumes flatten people into keywords.

Traditional resumes are static, compressed, and difficult to explore. They rarely show how a person thinks, how their work connects, or what evidence supports each skill.

PeerSlate is designed to make career information structured, searchable, interactive, and evidence-driven.

How It Works

From career history to interactive profile.

01

Capture the story

Professionals add career history, projects, skills, education, accomplishments, and goals through guided profile sections.

02

Structure the evidence

Skills, metrics, roles, and projects are connected so visitors can see what supports each claim.

03

Make it searchable

A profile-specific AI assistant answers questions using only approved public information and states limits when needed.

04

Help recruiters explore fit

Recruiters can view role-specific summaries, evidence, projects, and career direction instead of hunting through a static PDF.

Platform Direction

Built for professional evidence, not generic profiles.

Interactive portfolios

Personal branded pages with projects, timelines, evidence, and career context.

AI profile assistants

Chat assistants grounded in approved profile documents and structured public content.

Recruiter-focused views

Role-based filters that connect skills, results, experience, and project evidence.

Evidence-backed skills

Skills are connected to accomplishments, project artifacts, metrics, and outcomes.

Starting Point

Pete Carter's portfolio is the first working PeerSlate example.

The current portfolio demonstrates the early platform concept: structured resume data, interactive evidence, project storytelling, and an AI assistant grounded in approved career information.
