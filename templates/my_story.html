The Long Way Here

A path built on curiosity and grit.

From earning my engineering degree as a non-traditional student to leading high-impact programs in the Air Force and industry, my path has been anything but conventional. Every step shaped how I approach problems — bringing structure to chaos, aligning people toward a common goal, and delivering results that matter.

  1. The twenties

    Pizza, payroll, and people.

    Before engineering there were years managing Domino's Pizza stores — schedules, budgets, and teams under pressure. It's where leading people started, long before anyone called it leadership.

  2. The detour that mattered

    Running a healthcare company.

    Managing a healthcare business taught the stakes of getting operations right when people depend on you — and left a question that wouldn't go away: what else could I be capable of?

  3. Back to school at 36

    The bet on myself.

    I went back to school at 36 because I wanted goals I had once considered out of reach. Dalton State first, then a B.S. in Electrical Engineering at Kennesaw State — earned as a non-traditional student while life kept moving.

  4. 2021 – 2024 · Robins AFB

    A pandemic hire who learned fast.

    I joined the Air Force's 407th Supply Chain Management Squadron as a DoD civilian engineer in the middle of COVID — learning a complex engineering job largely over telework, then growing into Lead Systems Engineer for navigation-system sustainment and mission-critical special projects.

  5. 2024 – today · Industry

    L3Harris, then Northrop Grumman.

    Systems effectiveness at L3Harris; systems engineering, integration, and test at Northrop Grumman in Huntsville. Along the way: a master's in engineering management with a 4.0, and a PMP. The relationships built doing that work are among the accomplishments I value most.

  6. January 2027

    The next chapter is already inked.

    Admitted to the Systems Engineering Ph.D. program at the University of South Alabama. Still curious. Still climbing.
