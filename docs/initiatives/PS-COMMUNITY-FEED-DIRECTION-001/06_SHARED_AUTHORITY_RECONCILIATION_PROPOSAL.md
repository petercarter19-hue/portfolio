## 4. Reconciliation 1 — first-class Community Posts and contributions

### Historical authoritative statement

At the time of this analysis, the v2.9 Bible, v2.8 Roadmap, and former site
rules described a Feed item as a projection or reference derived from the
member's canonical Slate after Save Moment. Bible v2.9 also said a connection
update should link back to the member's Slate rather than become a detached
post object.

### Exact conflict

Pete approved an initial Community model in which a member can speak socially
and lightly without first creating private canonical history. Requiring every
ordinary post, question, image, reply, or author update to pass through Capture
and Journal would make Community depend on a different product workflow and
would contradict the locked composer and conversation model.

### Smallest proposed amendment

Amend shared authority to recognize two lawful Feed origins:

1. **Community-native:** a first-class `Community Post` authored and
   deliberately published to an allowed audience, with its own contributions,
   attachment, edit, moderation, revocation, and deletion lifecycle; and
2. **Slate-projected:** an explicit audience-governed projection or reference
   from an eligible canonical Slate record.

Add the following controlling boundary:

> A Community-native Post is authoritative for its own social content and
> conversation only. It is not canonical member history and does not create a
> Moment, enter Journal, alter a résumé, add to Story, create Project or Work
> truth, or broaden another audience. A later `Save Moment`, connection, or
> projection requires a separate explicit previewed member action and its own
> authorized contract. Feed cards, Replies & updates, selected contributions,
> conversations, rails, catch-up items, and counts remain derived views over
> the one applicable post/contribution source and never copy that source into a
> second social truth store.

This is an exception to the current statement that every Feed item must follow
Save Moment. It is not an exception to server-derived identity,
authorization-before-retrieval, private drafts, explicit audience choice,
moderation, revocation, deletion, provenance, or AI-proposal rules.

### Why necessary

- preserves ordinary low-friction social speech;
- prevents Journal from becoming a posting prerequisite;
- keeps Community and private canonical history as distinct data classes;
- allows explicit Slate projections when they are genuinely useful; and
- provides one authoritative source for each post and conversation.

### What remains unchanged

- Save Moment remains the only member-facing commit that creates a canonical
  Moment and immediate derived Journal membership.
- Community cannot automatically convert a post or reply into a Moment or
  another Slate object.
- AI cannot post, reply, link, save, publish, widen audience, or become the
  member's canonical voice.
- Exact audience tiers, publication transactions, relationship rules,
  moderation operations, and retention/deletion windows remain runtime-package
  decisions.

### Affected authority after approval

- Bible v2.9 sections 5 and 10, Appendix O, and `PS-CORE-USE-001`;
- Roadmap v2.8 target data layers, Phase 8, and the Feed work-package wording;
- site rules 2 and 23 plus a narrow Community-native Post rule in section 5;
- this package and the future dedicated Community runtime initiative; and
- current baseline/state/initiative pointers only when the approved shared
  amendments are activated.

### Implementation and release consequence

The later runtime package must separately define and prove canonical post and
contribution ownership, explicit audience selection, authorization before any
excerpt/count/media retrieval, moderation, attachments, edit/delete/revoke,
block/mute/report, two-member isolation, and optional exact-reference Slate
links. This proposal selects no schema or endpoint.

### Pete decision

**Approved, 2026-07-31.** The two-origin model is accepted, including the rule
that Community-native content never enters Journal or another Slate truth
store automatically.

## 5. Reconciliation 2 — desktop right rail

### Historical authoritative statement

Former site rules 26 and 27 reserved the Feed right rail for one member-owned
Note card and prohibited quote rails, challenges, people recommendations,
trends, polls, and decorative engagement furniture.

### Exact conflict

The Pete-locked desktop Feed has no Note card. Its right rail contains two
specific Community projections: `Community Pulse` and `Active Questions`.

### Smallest proposed replacement

Replace only the Community Feed application of rules 26 and 27 with:

> The first approved Community Feed desktop right rail contains exactly two
> bounded modules: Community Pulse and Active Questions. Community Pulse shows
> a small truthful summary of authorized Community movement. Active Questions
> shows a small set of current authorized question posts. Neither module may
> contain fabricated counts, popularity rank, trends, recommendations,
> challenges, quotes, polls, ads, or generic engagement filler. Each item links
> to its one authorized source, failures and empty states remain truthful, and
> the same source is not repeated merely to create density.
>
> The member-owned Note/Journal check-in pattern remains separately governed
> and is not part of the first locked Community Feed.

Recommended initial content budgets remain those prepared in the logical
architecture: no more than three Pulse rows and no more than three Active
Questions. Those caps may be tightened by the completed visual/state authority
but may not be expanded into scrolling mini-feeds without another owner
decision.

### Why necessary

- makes the accepted right rail legal without weakening the filler ban;
- gives ambient Community movement and open questions one clear location;
- preserves finite density and source truth; and
- prevents the old Note concept from competing with the locked Feed.

### What remains unchanged

- no quote, challenge, poll, people-recommendation, trend, job, ad, or
  popularity rail;
- no Ask Pete AI or member-specific public assistant in Community;
- no duplicate personal catch-up items from the left rail;
- no persistent mobile right rail; and
- no inference that a question or count is available without server evidence.

### Affected authority after approval

- site rules 26 and 27;
- the Roadmap Phase 8 Feed description and future package acceptance criteria;
- this package's completed visual-state authority; and
- the future Community runtime initiative.

### Implementation and release consequence

The runtime package must define authorized Pulse and Question projections,
deduplication, caps, freshness, empty/unavailable behavior, neutral permission
failures, privacy-safe counts, and mobile disposition before release.

### Pete decision

**Approved, 2026-07-31.** The exact two-module replacement is accepted, and the
Note card is not part of the first locked Community Feed.

## 6. Reconciliation 3 — Community-local left return rail

### Historical authoritative statement

The Context Rail standard permits a shared rail only for a room's genuine local
views, sections, filters, or tools. Its Community disposition anticipates a
later consolidation of local Community controls into that shared pattern.

### Exact conflict

The locked left rail is not a local-navigation control. It answers what changed
for the signed-in member and what they may resume through `Since you were
here`, `Continue the conversation`, `A Spark for you`, and caught-up context.
Calling it the Context Rail would violate the standard's purpose and would
incorrectly imply a shared component and mobile chip-row twin.

### Smallest proposed amendment

Amend only Community's disposition in the Context Rail standard and add a
narrow Community rule:

> The first locked Community Feed does not adopt the shared Context Rail. Its
> desktop left companion is a Community-local **return rail**, rendered as
> contextual content rather than navigation. It contains only the accepted
> owner-specific return functions, opens only authorized Community context or
> the Community composer, and creates no global route or navigation layer. It
> disappears on mobile and recomposes only through the Pete-locked Catch up and
> Spark sheet. It does not acquire the Context Rail skeleton, semantics, mobile
> chip row, or cross-room adoption authority.

This does not reject the Context Rail for every future Community design. Any
later adoption would require a new page-purpose decision showing that genuine
local views, sections, filters, or tools replace a weaker control.

### Why necessary

- preserves the accepted return experience without corrupting a shared
  navigation standard;
- prevents a second global navigation layer;
- preserves different jobs for left return context and right Community
  context; and
- keeps the mobile Catch up sheet as reflow, not a new permanent nav surface.

### What remains unchanged

- Context Rail Laws 1 and 2 remain intact for every adopting room.
- No rail entry may become a shortcut to Home/profile, Journal, Studio,
  Projects, Story, or another global destination.
- The left rail cannot become a notification firehose, second Feed, or
  engagement ranking.
- Spark creates, saves, and publishes nothing until an explicit member action.

### Affected authority after approval

- the Community row and a clarifying exception in the Context Rail standard;
- a narrow Community return-rail rule in the site rules;
- this package and the future Community runtime initiative; and
- no shared rail component or shell implementation by this decision alone.

### Implementation and release consequence

The future frontend must use distinct naming, semantics, and component
ownership for the return rail. It must prove owner-specific authorization,
deduplication, empty/partial-failure states, focus behavior, 200% reflow, and
the locked mobile sheet without modifying global navigation.

### Pete decision

**Approved, 2026-07-31.** The left surface is a Community-local return rail
that is explicitly not the Context Rail.
