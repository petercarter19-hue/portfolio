## 2. Locked visual and interaction constraints

### 2.1 Slate Board scale

- Preserve the corrected default board footprint in the locked pair: smaller
  than the first concept while remaining the dominant object.
- Preserve the zoom and expand/fullscreen controls so the member can move from
  calm overview to focused board work.
- Bring the selected-work panel higher in the page hierarchy so the board and
  active work feel like one workspace rather than two stacked products.
- On tablet, allow the board to use available width; on mobile, switch to a
  readable structured list/region view rather than shrink the spatial board.

### 2.2 Lines and relationship display

- Do not reintroduce the removed permanent horizontal/vertical crosshair or
  always-visible arrow network. The Board is exploratory, not a forced process
  diagram.
- Use headings, spacing, card position, and restrained background zoning to
  distinguish Work, Projects, Short Term, and Long Term.
- The faint relationship curves in the locked pair represent the **selected
  item state only**. They appear only on selection or an explicit
  show-connections action and disappear in the default/unselected state.
  Selection accent and matching detail treatment remain the primary cue.
- Do not draw a connector line from the board down to the detail panel. Matching
  selection treatment and title are sufficient.

### 2.3 Detail hierarchy and readability

- Preserve the corrected lower-border density and use spacing/headings before
  adding any further grid lines.
- Keep `What this is and why it matters`, source/evidence, and notes/backstory
  primary. Related uses, related future, and draft/published comparison remain
  secondary.
- Preserve the accepted `Draft wording and published wording` label.
- Increase the dark-theme contrast and minimum working type size in the detail
  panel and footer. Handwriting is an accent, not the only typeface for
  functional or long-form text.
- Preserve the four creative cards and contextual Ask Slate placement.

## 3. End-to-end member storyboard

| Scene | Member experience | Dominant action | Truth boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Arrive | The member enters Build Your Future from Workshop or Studio navigation and sees the private board, current focus, and published-Slate link. | Orient or select something already in motion. | Private workspace; no live content changes. |
| 2. Select | The member selects one grounded work, project, direction, or experiment card. Other relationship lines stay quiet. | Open this item. | Selection changes the workspace view only. |
| 3. Understand | The detail panel shows what the item is, why it matters, exact sources/evidence, notes, and its current draft/published relationship. | Inspect evidence or context. | Canonical facts remain source-linked; the panel does not copy or silently rewrite them. |
| 4. Change lens | The member views the same item through Work, Story, or Future. | See contribution, meaning, or possibility. | One source and one object; no parallel datasets or automatic placements. |
| 5. Develop | The member edits a private draft, adds evidence, writes/types/speaks, or uses optional Ask Slate assistance. | Develop this. | Direct editing works without AI; AI output is a reviewable proposal. |
| 6. Explore | The member tries another future, starts one time-boxed experiment, writes a future postcard, or gathers evidence. | Try one optional direction. | Optional, private, dismissible, non-predictive, and non-scoring. |
| 7. Reuse or practice | The member proposes a résumé/Work/Story/Project use or carries the grounded item into the current practice product. | Use in my Slate or practice. | No destination changes until the member reviews and confirms; current Interview Studio truth remains public/browser-local until a later package changes it. |
| 8. Leave and return | The member saves a private draft or leaves with nothing changed, then resumes from Workshop later. | Save privately, discard, or return. | The sandbox promise remains visible and enforced. |

## 6. The repeatable gate for every slice

Each slice follows the same loop:

1. **Map:** exact user story, source of truth, authorization boundary,
   lifecycle, unavailable states, forbidden files/capabilities, and homepage
   impact.
2. **Mock:** only that slice's full light/dark and responsive state set.
3. **Accept design:** Pete plus the designated manager approve the named visual
   authority before runtime work.
4. **Implement:** one writer, one branch, bounded files, direct/non-AI path
   first, no fabricated state.
5. **Verify:** focused contract tests, two-member isolation where private data
   exists, loading/empty/error/recovery, keyboard/touch, 200% reflow,
   reduced-motion, theme parity, and complete-diff self-review.
6. **Compare:** implementation screenshots against the exact authority and
   record every deviation.
7. **Accept and release:** owner/manager visual acceptance, Azure PR/squash,
   pipeline, production verification, and honest closeout.
8. **Then—and only then—open the next slice.**
