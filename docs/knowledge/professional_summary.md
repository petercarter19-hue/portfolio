# Professional Summary
Pete Carter is a systems engineer, engineering leader, and project-focused technical professional with more than five years of professional experience (beginning in 2020, after completing his Bachelor of Science in Electrical Engineering) across defense, aerospace, avionics sustainment, integrated logistics support, requirements management, systems integration, verification planning, lifecycle management, and cross-functional program execution. His career combines a Bachelor of Science in Electrical Engineering, a Master of Science in Engineering Management, project-management training, hands-on systems-engineering experience, and leadership responsibilities supporting complex, high-value government and aerospace programs.

Pete currently works as a Systems Engineer within Systems Engineering, Integration, and Test at Northrop Grumman in Huntsville, Alabama. In this role, he supports requirements development and management for the Sentinel program. His responsibilities include defining, decomposing, allocating, analyzing, validating, and maintaining system requirements; managing bidirectional requirements traceability in IBM DOORS; collaborating with engineering stakeholders; and supporting work execution through Agile practices, Jira, and Kanban-based workflows. This work strengthens his experience with complex system development, requirements architecture, technical integration, traceability, and disciplined engineering processes.

Before joining Northrop Grumman, Pete worked as a Systems Effectiveness Engineer on an Integrated Logistics Support team at L3Harris Technologies. His work supported defense and aerospace systems through sustainment engineering, product support analysis, logistics support analysis, spares planning, provisioning, reliability coordination, obsolescence management, depot planning, technical documentation, and lifecycle-support activities.

At L3Harris, Pete supported programs involving electronic warfare, communication, modem, and aviation-related systems. His responsibilities included developing and maintaining logistics-product data, analyzing supportability requirements, coordinating part-level provisioning information, evaluating spares requirements, supporting Diminishing Manufacturing Sources and Material Shortages activities, and collaborating with reliability, systems, supply-chain, procurement, manufacturing, and program-management personnel.

Pete has experience applying GEIA-STD-0007B concepts to logistics-product data and provisioning activities. He has helped translate technical product data, reliability information, operating assumptions, repair-cycle information, turnaround times, cost data, and supplier lead times into supportability recommendations and spares-planning decisions. His work has included H-table and B-table-related data, recommended spare-parts lists, provisioning documentation, depot-support planning, technical data packages, and Contract Data Requirements List deliverables.

He also supported program execution through Cost Account Management and Earned Value Management activities. This experience included monitoring cost and schedule performance, coordinating technical work, supporting estimates and planning, reviewing program risks, contributing to customer deliverables, and communicating status across engineering and program stakeholders. His background allows him to understand projects from both the technical and management perspectives rather than treating engineering and program execution as separate activities.

Earlier in his career, Pete served in progressively responsible engineering and leadership positions with the United States Department of Defense at Robins Air Force Base. He worked as a Systems Engineer and later as a Lead Systems Engineer and Lead Special Projects Engineer supporting the 407th Supply Chain Management Squadron. His work focused heavily on aircraft avionics, sustainment, systems engineering, supply-chain challenges, technical troubleshooting, modernization, requirements development, contracting support, and project leadership.

Pete supported avionics and sustainment efforts across multiple military aircraft platforms, including the C-130, C-5, C-17, F-15, F-16, B-52, and U-2. His work involved identifying systemic supportability problems, defining technical requirements, coordinating engineering investigations, developing acquisition and sustainment strategies, reviewing contractor performance, managing technical risks, and leading projects intended to improve aircraft availability and long-term system support.

As a government engineering leader, Pete led an engineering flight of more than 30 engineers and supported the coordination of engineering activities across multiple programs and technical disciplines. He oversaw nine redesign efforts with a combined value of approximately $19.2 million. These efforts required coordination among engineering teams, program offices, supply-chain personnel, contracting officers, sustainment organizations, equipment specialists, contractors, and senior leadership.

Pete served as the government point of contact for a $4.6 million B-52 ARN-67 glideslope modernization and redesign effort. His responsibilities included leading technical coordination, supporting requirements development, evaluating program risks, facilitating stakeholder communication, reviewing contractor progress, and helping guide the effort through government acquisition and engineering processes.

He also served as a Contracting Officer’s Representative for a $36 million Indefinite Delivery/Indefinite Quantity engineering-services contract. In this role, he monitored contractor execution, reviewed performance, coordinated technical requirements, supported task-order planning, maintained government oversight, and helped ensure that contracted engineering work aligned with program needs and contractual requirements.

His Department of Defense work included leading Integrated Product Team meetings, coordinating recurring program reviews, developing senior-leader briefing materials, supporting acquisition documentation, preparing Statements of Work and Performance Work Statements, reviewing engineering change proposals, managing technical action items, and communicating with both government and contractor organizations.

Pete’s technical and project experience includes avionics sustainment programs such as the APN-169 Station Keeping Equipment, ARN-67 glideslope system, ARN-112 Instrument Landing System, SSCVR, APS-133, and other navigation, communication, radar, and mission-support systems. He has worked on projects involving requirements definition, technical-data development, repair capability, test capability, parts shortages, obsolescence, redesigns, depot support, supply-chain performance, and lifecycle risk.

A major theme throughout Pete’s career has been the identification and resolution of problems that cross traditional organizational boundaries. Many of the issues he has addressed could not be solved by engineering, supply chain, contracting, logistics, or program management alone. His approach involves understanding the full system, identifying the responsible stakeholders, defining the problem clearly, organizing the work, tracking decisions and actions, evaluating risks, and guiding the team toward a supportable and measurable solution.

His work has contributed to significant operational and sustainment improvements. Examples include helping reduce part shortages by approximately 35 percent year over year through a sustainment tracker covering 54 avionics systems, reducing missing-parts issues by 52 percent, increasing repair and testing activity by 70 percent, reducing aged Awaiting Parts inventory by 69 percent, and helping eliminate a backlog of Mission Impaired Capability Awaiting Parts conditions from 16 to zero on a supported system.
